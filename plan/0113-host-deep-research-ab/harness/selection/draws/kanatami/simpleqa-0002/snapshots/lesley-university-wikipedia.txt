**Lesley University** is a [private university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_university "Private university") in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts "Cambridge, Massachusetts"), United States. It was founded in 1909 to [educate teachers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_school "Normal school"). While founded as a [women's college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_college "Women's college"), male students were admitted beginning in 2005\.

## History

### Lesley School and College (1909–2001)

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Lesley_University&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Lesley School and College (1909–2001)")\]

The **Lesley School** (also known as **Lesley Normal School**) was founded by [Edith Lesley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edith_Lesley "Edith Lesley") in 1909 at her home at 29 Everett Street, Cambridge. The school began as a private women's institution that trained [kindergarten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindergarten "Kindergarten") teachers. It espoused the work of [Friedrich Froebel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Fröbel "Friedrich Fröbel"), who invented the concept of kindergarten as a complement to the care given to children by their mothers. Teacher and writer Elizabeth Peabody opened Boston's first Froebel-inspired kindergarten in 1860; more kindergartens followed. Central to the Froebelian philosophy is the idea that individuals are important and unique, a focus that remains at Lesley University today.

Edith Lesley, after living in Panama and Maine and studying in Freiburg, Germany, moved to Boston and became involved in public school teaching. She completed kindergarten training, took courses at Radcliffe College, and then began to plan her own kindergarten training school. She wanted a school that would "consider the individual of basic importance; to inculcate the idea of gracious living; and to foster the tradition of American democracy."[\[10\]](#cite_note-10) Once married, Lesley and her husband expanded the school by constructing an addition at the rear of their home, which today is known as Livingston Stebbins Hall. Around 1913, the Lesley School began training elementary teachers.

In 1941, the Lesley School reorganized under a [board of trustees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_trustees "Board of trustees"); in 1944, it received authority to award [baccalaureate degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree "Bachelor's degree") and became known as **Lesley College**. In 1954, the college began to award graduate degrees; it later added majors in the fields of [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education "Education"), [counseling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counseling "Counseling"), [human services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_services "Human services"), [global studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_studies "Global studies"), [art therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_therapy "Art therapy"), and [management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management "Management").

### School of Practical Art and The Art Institute of Boston (1912–1998)

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Lesley_University&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: School of Practical Art and The Art Institute of Boston (1912–1998)")\]

The **School of Practical Art** was founded by Roy Davidson in 1912\. The school's early philosophy was based upon [John Ruskin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ruskin "John Ruskin")'s words that it is "in art that the heart, the head, and the hand of a man come together" and Davidson's own belief that "[beauty comes from the use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Form_follows_function "Form follows function")."[\[11\]](#cite_note-11) The school increasingly embraced the [fine arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_arts "Fine arts") and developed a growing [liberal arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_arts "Liberal arts") curriculum; in 1967 the school was renamed **The Art Institute of Boston** **(AIB)** to acknowledge its increased focus upon fine art as well as design, illustration, and photography. In 1998, The Art Institute of Boston and Lesley College merged,[\[12\]](#cite_note-12) setting the stage for the eventual formation of Lesley University, where AIB would become **Lesley College of Art and Design**.

### Founding of Lesley University and early expansion (2001–2013)

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Lesley_University&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Founding of Lesley University and early expansion (2001–2013)")\]

Lesley became Lesley University in 2001\. When university status was granted, the original colleges became the university's undergraduate units, while Lesley College's two graduate schools rounded out the university's four main academic units. In 2005, Lesley College (at that point, an all-female liberal arts college) became [coeducational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coeducation "Coeducation").

In 2006, the university acquired [Prospect Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospect_Hall_\(Cambridge,_Massachusetts\) "Prospect Hall (Cambridge, Massachusetts)"), a former church listed on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places "National Register of Historic Places"), with the goal of bringing The Art Institute of Boston to Cambridge.[\[13\]](#cite_note-13) In 2007, Joseph B. Moore became president of Lesley. The following year, the university entered into a partnership with [Episcopal Divinity School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Divinity_School "Episcopal Divinity School") to jointly operate their Brattle Street campus and purchase several buildings. This move added dormitories, a dining hall, and classrooms, as well as expanded library services and administrative space.[\[14\]](#cite_note-14)

In 2009, the university celebrated its centennial and embarked on its first major construction since the 1970s. Dormitories at 1 and 3 Wendell Street were added to the residential life offerings; both buildings are [LEED Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leadership_in_Energy_and_Environmental_Design "Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design")–certified.[\[15\]](#cite_note-15)

### Consolidation in Cambridge (2013–2018)

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Lesley_University&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Consolidation in Cambridge (2013–2018)")\]

In 2013, construction on the Lunder Arts Center began in Porter Square. The project was built on the former site of the [North Prospect Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Prospect_Church "North Prospect Church"), which was moved slightly to the south and repurposed.[\[16\]](#cite_note-16) Also in 2013, Lesley University's constituent colleges, The Art Institute of Boston and Lesley College, were renamed **College of Art and Design** and **College of Liberal Arts and Sciences**, respectively; the change was reflective of the cohesion and growth of the two colleges.[\[17\]](#cite_note-17)

In 2015, the College of Art and Design officially left Kenmore Square[\[18\]](#cite_note-18) in Boston and joined the remainder of the university in Cambridge. This move marked the completion of the Lunder Arts Center and was the first time in 17 years that the university was entirely housed in Cambridge. The Lunder Arts Center was awarded a LEED Gold certification[\[19\]](#cite_note-19) from the [U.S. Green Building Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Green_Building_Council "U.S. Green Building Council"). Lesley also won a Preservation Award[\[20\]](#cite_note-20) from the Cambridge Historical Commission for the restoration of the historic former North Prospect Church as part of the Lunder Arts Center project.

At the end of the 2014–15 academic year, President Joseph B. Moore announced that he would retire the following year.[\[5\]](#cite_note-bostonglobe.com-5) In 2016, Jeff A. Weiss[\[21\]](#cite_note-21) became president; he resigned in 2018 due to personal health reasons and almost immediately became the Chief Strategy and Transformation Officer at Mass General Brigham.[\[22\]](#cite_note-22) Richard S. Hansen became interim president later that year.[\[23\]](#cite_note-23)

In July 2018, Lesley announced the purchase of the historic buildings formerly owned by the Episcopal Divinity School (EDS), making Lesley the sole owner of the 4\.4-acre Brattle Campus.[\[24\]](#cite_note-24) The purchase included five buildings – St. John's Memorial Chapel, Wright Hall, Burnham Hall, Reed Hall, and 4 Berkeley St. – and the remainder of Sherrill Hall.[\[25\]](#cite_note-25) Since 2008, Lesley and EDS had jointly owned Sherrill Hall as part of the schools' condominium agreement.

### Recent years (2019–present)

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Lesley_University&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Recent years (2019–present)")\]

Janet L. Steinmayer became university president in July 2019\.[\[26\]](#cite_note-26) Since 2019, the faculty has passed three no-confidence votes against university leadership.[\[27\]](#cite_note-kohli-27)

In 2021, Lesley briefly piloted a community-based partnership with DeMello International Center in [New Bedford, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Bedford,_Massachusetts "New Bedford, Massachusetts").[\[28\]](#cite_note-28)

In fall 2023, Lesley laid off 30 faculty members and 20 staff, largely from the undergraduate College of Liberal Arts and Sciences. The university also eliminated some ostensibly low-enrolled academic programs, including all social science majors, and announced an overall restructuring of the university, branded as "Better Lesley."[\[29\]](#cite_note-crunch-29) As of 2024, enrollment had dropped to less than half its 2014 level.[\[27\]](#cite_note-kohli-27)

Strikes at Lesley University regarding Steinmeyer's presidency made news shortly before her choice to not renew her contract. They were covered by local networks such as The Boston Globe[\[30\]](#cite_note-30), NBC Boston[\[31\]](#cite_note-31), The Cambridge Somerville Independent[\[32\]](#cite_note-32)[\[33\]](#cite_note-33) and The Cambridge Day[\[34\]](#cite_note-34). These strikes were organized by the university's staff union with SEIU509[\[35\]](#cite_note-35) and were supported by the student body, with many students choosing to join the protesting outside of University Hall and the Lunder Arts Center.

Lesley University is [accredited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education_accreditation_in_the_United_States "Higher education accreditation in the United States") by the [New England Commission of Higher Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Commission_of_Higher_Education "New England Commission of Higher Education").[\[36\]](#cite_note-36)The university, with its component undergraduate colleges, graduate schools, and centers, offers more than 20 undergraduate majors and over 90 Adult Bachelor's, Master's, Certificates of Advanced Graduate Study, and PhD programs at its Cambridge and Boston campuses, as well as off-campus and online. The Lesley Center for the Adult Learner offers an adult bachelor's degree program, including on- and off-campus courses as well as online and hybrid courses targeted toward adult learners.

The university is made up of the following academic units:[\[37\]](#cite_note-37)

- College of Art and Design

- College of Liberal Arts and Sciences

  - Center for the Adult Learner

- Graduate School of Education

  - Center for Reading Recovery & Literacy Collaborative

- Graduate School of Arts and Social Sciences

- Threshold Program

The university library system is made up of the following units:[\[38\]](#cite_note-38)

- Henry Knox Sherrill Library – Main collections

  - Teaching Resources Collection

  - Evelyn M. Finnegan '48 Collection for Children's Literature[\[39\]](#cite_note-39)

- University Archives

- John and Carol Moriarty Library – Lunder Arts Center

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Lesley_University&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Campuses")\]

### South Campus

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Lesley_University&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: South Campus")\]

The South Campus, formerly called the Brattle Campus, is on [Brattle Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brattle_Street_\(Cambridge,_Massachusetts\) "Brattle Street (Cambridge, Massachusetts)") in the [Old Cambridge Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Cambridge_Historic_District "Old Cambridge Historic District"), just west of [Harvard Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Square "Harvard Square").[\[40\]](#cite_note-40) It is the former site of the [Episcopal Divinity School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Divinity_School "Episcopal Divinity School"). Lesley purchased the property completely in 2018, having previously shared it with Episcopal Divinity School.[\[41\]](#cite_note-41) At some point between 2018–2020, it was renamed "South Campus".

It is home to four residence halls, a dining hall, classrooms, the Sherrill Hall, and the Graduate School of Arts and Social Sciences—that building is also the birthplace of [Charles Sanders Peirce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Sanders_Peirce "Charles Sanders Peirce").\[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*\]

### Doble Campus

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Lesley_University&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Doble Campus")\]

The Doble Campus is between [Harvard Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Square "Harvard Square") and [Porter Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porter_Square "Porter Square"). It is named for Lesley benefactor and former chair of the Lesley Corporation, Frank C. Doble.[\[42\]](#cite_note-doble-42)

It is home to residence halls and a dining hall, classrooms, and the College of Liberal Arts and Sciences,[\[42\]](#cite_note-doble-42) as well as Marran Theater and a variety of administrative offices. It is also home to many student life facilities, such as the Margaret McKenna Student Center, the Information Commons (a 24-hour computer lab and study space), and the fitness center.

### Porter Campus

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Lesley_University&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Porter Campus")\]

The Porter Campus is in [Porter Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porter_Square "Porter Square").

It is home to the majority of the university's classroom space, the College of Art and Design, the Lunder Arts Center, the Graduate School of Education, as well as Student Administrative and Financial Services, the university bookstore, the Moriarty Library and the majority of the university's art galleries.\[*[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*\]\[*[when?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")*\]
