Scottish mathematician and manufacturer of optical instruments

James Short [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society")

Education | [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh "University of Edinburgh")

James Short's reflecting telescope

**James Short** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society") (10 June [O.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates "Old Style and New Style dates") (21 June [N.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates "Old Style and New Style dates")) 1710 – 14 June 1768) was a Scottish [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematician "Mathematician") and manufacturer of [optical instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_instrument "Optical instrument"), principally [telescopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescope "Telescope"). During his 35-year career as a telescope-maker he produced approximately 1,360 scientific instruments.[\[1\]](#cite_note-1)

## Early life and education

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=James_Short_\(mathematician\)&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life and education")\]

Short was born in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh "Edinburgh") in 1710 to Margaret Grierson and William Short, a [carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright "Wright"). When he was orphaned at about the age of 10, he was accepted into the [Heriot's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Heriot's_School#History "George Heriot's School"), an orphanage, and at 12 transferred to the [Royal High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_High_School,_Edinburgh "Royal High School, Edinburgh") where he excelled in the study of the [classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classics "Classics"). In 1726 he entered the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh "University of Edinburgh") to study [divinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divinity_\(academic_discipline\) "Divinity (academic discipline)"), however after being inspired by lectures given by professor of [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") [Colin Maclaurin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Maclaurin "Colin Maclaurin"), he transferred to [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy "Astronomy") and mathematics.[\[2\]](#cite_note-DNB-2)

## Telescope manufacture

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=James_Short_\(mathematician\)&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Telescope manufacture")\]

In 1732 Maclaurin gave Short access to use his rooms in the university to work on for experiments in the construction of telescopes. Such was the quality of Short's instruments that in recognition of his skill he was elected as a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society "Royal Society") on 24 March 1737\.[\[2\]](#cite_note-DNB-2) In Short's first telescopes the [specula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror "Mirror") were made of glass, as suggested by [James Gregory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gregory_\(astronomer_and_mathematician\) "James Gregory (astronomer and mathematician)"), however later he used metallic specula only, and thus succeeded in giving them true [parabolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraboloid "Paraboloid") and [elliptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellipsoid "Ellipsoid") shapes.

Short then adopted telescope-making as his profession, which he practised first in Edinburgh up until 1738, after which he transferred to [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London").

Almost all of Short's telescopes were of the [Gregorian form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_telescope "Gregorian telescope"), and some of them even today retain their original high polish and sharp definition.

In 1736 [Queen Caroline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_of_Ansbach "Caroline of Ansbach") requested him to instruct her second son, [William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_William,_Duke_of_Cumberland "Prince William, Duke of Cumberland"), in mathematics.

In March 1737 Short was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society "Fellow of the Royal Society")[\[3\]](#cite_note-3) and in 1758 became a foreign member of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences "Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences"). He was a founder member of the [Society for the Encouragement of Arts, Manufactures and Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_the_Encouragement_of_Arts,_Manufactures_and_Commerce "Society for the Encouragement of Arts, Manufactures and Commerce") in 1754\.

Short died in [Newington Butts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newington_Butts "Newington Butts"), London in 1768, having made a considerable fortune from his profession.[\[2\]](#cite_note-DNB-2)
