# Who first built a Gregorian reflecting telescope?

## Answer

**Robert Hooke** was the first person to build a working Gregorian reflecting
telescope, completing it in 1673.
([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_telescope))

## Details and corroboration

The Gregorian design is named after the Scottish mathematician and astronomer
James Gregory, who published it in his 1663 work *Optica Promota*, but he never
actually constructed the instrument himself.
([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_telescope))

Gregory's own attempts to build it failed because he lacked the practical skill
and could find no optician able to make the mirrors; it was only about ten years
after publication, "aided by the interest of experimental scientist Robert
Hooke," that a working instrument was finally created.
([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_telescope))

Oxford's History of Science Museum records that "it wasn't until 10 years later
that he [Gregory] was able to recruit Robert Hooke to help him build it — five
years after Newton ha[d] successfully built his own reflecting telescope."
([hsm.ox.ac.uk/gregorian-telescope](https://www.hsm.ox.ac.uk/gregorian-telescope))

Hooke's biography likewise credits him with the construction: dissatisfied with
refracting telescopes, "he built the first practical Gregorian telescope that
used a silvered glass mirror."
([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hooke))

## Note on James Short

The Scottish optician James Short later became famous for mass-producing
Gregorian telescopes with polished speculum-metal mirrors in the 18th century,
but he did not build the first one — that was Robert Hooke in 1673.
([en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_telescope))
