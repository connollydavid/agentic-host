# Research Report: Who accompanied Lilian Ngoyi on the illegal journey to Lausanne?

## Answer

**Dora Tamana** (Dora Ntloko Tamana, 1901–1983), the South African anti-apartheid activist, trade unionist, Communist Party member, and National Secretary of the Federation of South African Women (FEDSAW), accompanied Lilian Ngoyi on the illegal 1955 journey to Lausanne, Switzerland, for the World Congress of Mothers of the Women's International Democratic Federation (WIDF).

The research-deep workflow was adapted (no `outline.yaml` existed in the working directory): a background web-search agent was launched and four pages were fetched directly and verified. ## Findings

### The journey and the companion

The Wikipedia article on Ngoyi states that Ngoyi "embarked on an illegal journey to Lausanne, Switzerland" to attend the WIDF's World Congress of Mothers (S1, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilian_Ngoyi). The same paragraph names her traveling companion: "Accompanied by her fellow activist Dora Tamana, and as an official delegate of FEDSAW, she embarked on a journey that would see an attempt to stow away on a boat leaving Cape Town under 'white names'..." (S1, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilian_Ngoyi).

The Women's History Network article by historian Nicholas Grant independently confirms this: Ngoyi "embarked on an audacious (and highly illegal) journey to Lausanne, Switzerland in 1955 to participate in the World Congress of Mothers held by the Women's International Democratic Federation (WIDF)", and was "Accompanied by her fellow activist Dora Tamana, and as an official delegate of FEDSAW" (S4, https://web.archive.org/web/20110902142904/http://womenshistorynetwork.org/blog/?tag=nicholas-grant).

South African History Online corroborates from Ngoyi's side: "Together with Dora Tamana, she was arrested while trying to board a ship to a conference in Switzerland without a passport" (S3, https://www.sahistory.org.za/people/lilian-masediba-ngoyi).

The Wikipedia article on Tamana corroborates from Tamana's side: "The Federation of South African Women chose two delegates to attend the Congress of Mothers organized by the Women's International Democratic Federation (WIDF) in 1955. Dora Tamana accompanies fellow delegate and founder of FEDSAW, Lilian Ngoyi to the Congress" (S2, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dora_Tamana).

### Why the journey was illegal

Neither woman had a valid passport (S2, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dora_Tamana). Ngoyi was denied a passport because of her ANC and trade-union memberships (S1, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilian_Ngoyi). According to Tamana's article, "Neither she nor Ngoyi had official passports since it was hard for women associated within this work to obtain one. Tamana and Ngoyi stowed away on a boat leaving Cape Town for Britain under false identities" (S2, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dora_Tamana). SAHO likewise records that the pair tried to board a ship to the Switzerland conference "without a passport" and were arrested in the attempt (S3, https://www.sahistory.org.za/people/lilian-masediba-ngoyi).

### Who was Dora Tamana

Dora Ntloko Tamana (11 November 1901 – 23 July 1983) was a "prominent South African anti-apartheid activist" and a member of the Communist Party of South Africa; she became National Secretary of FEDSAW in 1954 (S2, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dora_Tamana). FEDSAW's international relationship with the WIDF "allowed her to go to the World Congress of Mothers in Lausanne, Switzerland in 1955" (S2, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dora_Tamana).

### Aftermath

"Lausanne was their primary destination but the two FEDSAW delegates were able to travel together to other locations like Germany, Switzerland, Romania, China, and Russia" (S2, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dora_Tamana). At Lausanne, Ngoyi "presided over the 2nd session of the conference, giving its opening address" (S4, https://web.archive.org/web/20110902142904/http://womenshistorynetwork.org/blog/?tag=nicholas-grant). Grant argues the trip "effectively asserted Ngoyi's right, as a black woman, to travel and move freely" and that by escaping the pass laws, "these two black women struck at the heart of the doctrine of apartheid" (S4, https://web.archive.org/web/20110902142904/http://womenshistorynetwork.org/blog/?tag=nicholas-grant). After returning, Tamana "was banned by the South African government from attending political meetings for five years" (S2, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dora_Tamana), and Ngoyi received her first banning order in 1962 (S4, https://web.archive.org/web/20110902142904/http://womenshistorynetwork.org/blog/?tag=nicholas-grant).

## Note on dates

Sources date the journey to 1955 (S1, S2, S3, S4); some accounts also associate the trip with the period before the 9 August 1956 women's march. All four fetched sources agree on Dora Tamana as the companion.
