Evidence read 2026-11-27. (1) English Wikipedia, "Seine" (revision 1384649318): "The Seine ... is a 777-kilometre-long (483 mi) river in northern France. It rises at Source-Seine, 30 kilometres (19 mi) northwest of Dijon in northeastern France in the Langres plateau, flowing through Paris and into the English Channel at Le Havre (and Honfleur on the left bank)." Also: "There are 37 bridges in Paris across the Seine", and the Seine's average depth in Paris is "approximately 9.5 meters (31 feet)"; the Paris section is one of five named parts of the course, the *Traversée de Paris*.
(2) UNESCO, "Paris, Banks of the Seine": "The city of Paris is built along a bend in the River Seine, between the confluence of the Marne and the Oise Rivers", and "Paris is a river city. Ever since the first human settlements, from prehistoric times to the Parisii tribes, the Seine has played both a defensive and an economic role."

Nuance: the Seine is the river *of* Paris, but other named watercourses also touch the metropolitan area — the Marne joins the Seine just downstream at Charenton-le-Pont, and the Bièvre, a former left-bank tributary inside Paris, is now culverted. The Canal Saint-Martin joins the Seine in Paris and is a canal, not a river. Volatility: stable (physical geography).
