Read 2026-11-27 from en.wikisource.org, "Constitution of the Fifth French Republic (amended, 2008)" (page revision 1757684; the page itself notes "This work is not backed by a scanned copy of the edition from which it was transcribed"). Article 1 as displayed: "France shall be an indivisible, secular, democratic and social Republic. It shall ensure the equality of all citizens before the law, without distinction of origin, race or religion. It shall respect all beliefs. It shall be organised on a decentralised basis. Statutes shall promote equal access by women and men to elective offices and posts as well as to professional and social positions." Article 2 lists language, emblem, anthem and maxim, but no capital.

Gap: the fetched page was cut off at line 1066 of 1380 (around Article 65), so Titles XII and later were not read; the absence of a capital clause in the remainder is unverified. Do not use this note to assert that the French Constitution never mentions Paris. Attempted primary-source corroboration failed: www.britannica.com/place/Paris returned HTTP 403 and www.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/la-constitution-de-la-cinquieme-republique returned HTTP 404 on the same date.

Confidence raised from unverified to checked on 2026-11-27: the coordinator read this Wikisource page directly and Article 1 and Article 2 as displayed contain no capital clause, so the claim holds as stated with its scope limit (read stopped near Article 65). Not `verified` because the transcription is flagged on the page as not backed by a scanned source and the tail of the document was not read.
