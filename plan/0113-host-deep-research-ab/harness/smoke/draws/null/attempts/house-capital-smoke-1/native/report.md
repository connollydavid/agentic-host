# What is the capital of France, and which river runs through it?

**Short answer:** The capital of France is **Paris**, and the river that runs through it is the **Seine**.

## The capital: Paris

Paris is the capital and largest city of France, and the article lead states this in its first sentence (https://en.wikipedia.org/api/rest_v1/page/summary/Paris).
> "Paris is the capital and largest city of France, with an estimated city population of 2.04 million in an area of 105.4 km2 (40.7 sq mi), as of January 2026, and a metropolitan population of 13.3 million (2023). Located on the Seine in the centre of the Île-de-France region, it is the largest metropolitan area and fourth-most populous city in the European Union (EU)." (https://en.wikipedia.org/api/rest_v1/page/summary/Paris)

The same lead appears in the full article, which also records that Paris took on that role in the Middle Ages: "By the end of the 12th century, Paris had become the political, economic, religious, and cultural capital of France." (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris)

Because it is the capital, the seat of the French national government is in the city: the article's "National government" section opens with the words "As the capital of France" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris). A general reference work puts the capital and the river in one line: "Paris is the largest city and the capital city of France. Paris is on the River Seine, or the Seine River." (https://www.worldatlas.com/articles/which-river-runs-through-paris.html)

The capital status is also reflected in the UNESCO listing for the riverside property, which describes "the different layers of the history of Paris, the capital city of one of the first great nation states of Europe" (https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/600/).

## The river: the Seine

The Seine is the river that crosses the city: "The city of Paris is built along a bend in the River Seine, between the confluence of the Marne and the Oise Rivers." (https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/600/)

The French encyclopaedia article on the river confirms the route: "It rises at Source-Seine, 30 kilometres (19 mi) northwest of Dijon in northeastern France in the Langres plateau, flowing through Paris and into the English Channel at Le Havre." (https://en.wikipedia.org/api/rest_v1/page/summary/Seine)

The city itself confirms the length of the river's urban course, 13 km of the river's 777 km total: "13 km (sur un total de 777 km de longueur entre le plateau de Langres et l'estuaire du Havre)" (https://www.paris.fr/dossiers/la-seine-coeur-battant-de-paris-216). The same page introduces the Seine as the river that "fait vibrer la capitale" (makes the capital vibrate) along its 37 bridges spread over the 13 kilometres it runs intra-muros (https://www.paris.fr/dossiers/la-seine-coeur-battant-de-paris-216).

Within Paris the river is crossed and channelled rather than merely passing by: "Paris is spread widely on both banks of the river." (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris) The crossing count is confirmed on the river's own article: "At Paris, there are 37 bridges." (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine)

## Supporting detail on the river

- The Seine is a major northern French river: "The Seine is a 777-kilometre-long (483 mi) river in northern France." (https://en.wikipedia.org/api/rest_v1/page/summary/Seine)
- It is economically and recreationally tied to the capital: the article's lead notes that excursion boats "offer sightseeing tours of the river banks in the capital city, Paris" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine)
- Its course inside the city is short relative to its total length: "The River Seine flows 8.078 miles through the city from east to west." (https://www.worldatlas.com/articles/which-river-runs-through-paris.html)
- Its human pressure is concentrated on the capital region: "the Seine-Normandy watershed experiences the highest human impacts of any hydrographic basin in France" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine)
- The stretch through central Paris has been protected as World Heritage since 1991, described as "an exceptional and unique example of urban riverside architecture" (https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/600/)
