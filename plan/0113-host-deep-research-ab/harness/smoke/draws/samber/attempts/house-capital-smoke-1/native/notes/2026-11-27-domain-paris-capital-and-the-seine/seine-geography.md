# Research notes — axis: the principal river running through the capital of France

Research date: 2026-11-27. Scope: Metropolitan France, present day. Question answered: (1) Which river runs through Paris? — the Seine. (2) Supporting hydrographic facts.

## River running through the capital — the Seine

### Identification

The river that runs through Paris, the capital of France, is the **Seine** (French: *la Seine*). The French interior ministry's official geography atlas describes it as rising "en Bourgogne, près de Dijon" and emptying into the English Channel (la Manche), with a length given as 776 kilometres [Atlas des fleuves français, Ministère de l'Intérieur](https://formation-civique.interieur.gouv.fr/fiches-par-thematiques/histoire-geographie-et-culture/atlas-de-la-france/les-fleuves-fran%C3%A7ais/) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary). The City of Paris presents the Seine as its own river in official communications — "La Seine, cœur battant de Paris" — stating it runs 777 km in total "entre le plateau de Langres et l'estuaire du Havre" [Ville de Paris](https://www.paris.fr/dossiers/la-seine-coeur-battant-de-paris-216) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary). In its UNESCO World Heritage description, Paris is explicitly named "the capital city of one of the first great nation states of Europe," built "along a bend in the River Seine, between the confluence of the Marne and the Oise Rivers" [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, ref. 600](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/600/) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary). The name derives from *Sequana*, the Gallo-Roman/Celtic goddess of the river, whose sanctuary stood at the source [Ville de Paris](https://www.paris.fr/pages/5-choses-a-savoir-sur-les-sources-de-la-seine-28197) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary); Britannica's students' encyclopedia adds that the French pronunciation is /sɛn/ and that the Seine is France's second-longest river after the Loire [Britannica Students](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Seine-River/276976) (accessed via search-engine excerpt 2026-11-27, full page returned HTTP 403; confidence: Medium, tier: Established).

### Source

The Seine rises in the commune of **Source-Seine** (Côte-d'Or department, Bourgogne-Franche-Comté), on the **Plateau de Langres**, about 30 km northwest of Dijon [Britannica Students](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Seine-River/276976) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Established; corroborated by [Ville de Paris](https://www.paris.fr/pages/la-seine-aurait-plus-de-14-000-ans-et-autres-anecdotes-surprenantes-sur-le-fleuve-parisien-19981), tier: Primary, and [Agence de l'eau Seine-Normandie](https://www.eau-seine-normandie.fr/agence-de-leau/le-bassin-de-la-seine), tier: Primary). The source elevation is reported inconsistently: the public water agency AESN gives 446 m on its climate page and "450 mètres" on its basin page [AESN](https://www.eau-seine-normandie.fr/La-Seine-sous-l-effet-du-changement-climatique) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary), Ville de Paris gives 447 m [Ville de Paris](https://www.paris.fr/pages/la-seine-aurait-plus-de-14-000-ans-et-autres-anecdotes-surprenantes-sur-le-fleuve-parisien-19981) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary), while the NGO Initiatives Rivers' technical synopsis gives 452 m [Initiatives Rivers synopsis sheet, PDF](https://www.initiativesrivers.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Synopsis-sheet-SEINE_EN_31012021.pdf) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: Medium, tier: Established, NGO editorial process) and Paris's own heritage-service site recalls the "schoolbook" figure of 471 m while treating it as outdated [Patrimoine professionnel, Ville de Paris](https://patpro.paris.fr/lieux/les-sources-de-la-seine-un-parc-parisien-en-pleine-campagne) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). The cluster of recent official values is 446–452 m; the ~471 m figure appears to be a legacy value.

A notable administrative fact: the source site is **owned by the City of Paris itself**. The Paris municipal council voted its acquisition on 6 April 1864, under Baron Haussmann, buying a valley of just over one hectare for 12,500 francs; a landscaped park with an artificial grotto designed by Victor Baltard and Gabriel Davioud was inaugurated in 1867, crowned by a statue of the nymph Sequana (original by François Jouffroy, replaced in 1934 by a copy sculpted by Paul Auban). The City remains the owner today [Ville de Paris](https://www.paris.fr/pages/5-choses-a-savoir-sur-les-sources-de-la-seine-28197) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary; corroborated by [Ville de Paris exhibition panels, PDF](https://cdn.paris.fr/paris/2024-12-12/panneaux-exposources-de-la-seine_v9-light-WIg2.pdf), tier: Primary). The site is an ancient healing sanctuary: a Gallo-Roman shrine to Sequana (first century BC onward) whose vestiges, found from 1836, include nearly a thousand bronze, stone and wood ex-voto kept at the Musée archéologique de Dijon; a new Inrap excavation programme for 2026–2029 is under way with the City of Paris's support [Ville de Paris](https://www.paris.fr/pages/5-choses-a-savoir-sur-les-sources-de-la-seine-28197) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary). The same Langres plateau village area also gives birth to the Marne, the Aube and the Meuse [Ville de Paris](https://www.paris.fr/pages/la-seine-aurait-plus-de-14-000-ans-et-autres-anecdotes-surprenantes-sur-le-fleuve-parisien-19981) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). River distance from the sources to Paris is given as 288 km [Ville de Paris](https://www.paris.fr/pages/5-choses-a-savoir-sur-les-sources-de-la-seine-28197) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary), though another City page says the source lies "à 231 kilomètres de la capitale" (straight-line vs river distance — see Conflicts).

### Length, course, direction, and territory crossed

**Length.** Sources disagree. The Sandre national water-reference database (the French official reference, code ----0010) records 775 km [Sandre/EauFrance, Wayback capture of 2021-01-23 of https://sandre.eaufrance.fr/geo/CoursEau/----0010](https://web.archive.org/web/20210123171121/http:/www.sandre.eaufrance.fr/geo/CoursEau/----0010) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary; live page is now JS-only and could not be scraped directly). The interior ministry atlas says 776 km (tier: Primary, above); Universalis (written by a physical geographer) says 776 km [Encyclopædia Universalis](https://www.universalis.fr/encyclopedie/seine/) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Established); Ville de Paris and AESN's climate page say 777 km (tier: Primary, above); AESN's basin page says the river reaches the sea "773 kilomètres plus loin" from the source [AESN](https://www.eau-seine-normandie.fr/agence-de-leau/le-bassin-de-la-seine) (confidence: Medium, tier: Primary); the peer-reviewed UNESCO-published chapter by Lestel, Meybeck, Carré and Belliard (Sorbonne/INRAE) gives 775 km "from its headwaters to its mouth," decomposed as 607 km river proper plus a 170-km fluvial/saline estuary [Lestel et al., "The Seine, the River dedicated to Paris", in *River culture*, UNESCO Publishing 2023](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366871446_The_Seine_the_River_dedicated_to_Paris) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary); Britannica Students gives 485 mi = 781 km (tier: Established, above); and the European Commission JRC basin fact-sheet uses 754 km [JRC FACT SHEET: Seine River Basin, PDF](https://water.jrc.ec.europa.eu/pdf/seine-fs.pdf) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary). Synthesis: the overwhelmingly common official figure is **775–777 km**; outliers (754–781 km) reflect different datasets, measurement dates, and whether the tidal estuary reach is counted.

**Direction and route.** The Seine's general orientation is south-east to north-west ("une orientation générale du sud-est au nord-ouest"), from the Langres plateau to the Channel [commune of Rives-en-Seine](https://www.rives-en-seine.fr/patrimoine-naturel/la-seine/) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary) and Universalis junior likewise: "trajectoire du sud-est vers le nord-ouest" traversing Champagne, the Île-de-France and Normandy [Universalis Junior](https://junior.universalis.fr/encyclopedie/seine) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: Medium, tier: Established); within Paris it effectively runs east→west [Paris je t'aime, official tourism board](https://parisjetaime.com/article/se-reperer-a-paris-plan-de-paris-a948) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary). It drains the sedimentary Paris Basin (a geological lowland), on a low-slope, low-energy course (average slope ~20 cm/km, sandy bed) with many meander loops ("boucles") [Lestel et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366871446_The_Seine_the_River_dedicated_to_Paris) (tier: Primary) and AESN's description of the "bassin sédimentaire de Paris" as stacked "hollow plates" [AESN](https://www.eau-seine-normandie.fr/agence-de-leau/le-bassin-de-la-seine) (tier: Primary). Britannica Students describes the course as taking in the Aube at Romilly, the Yonne at Montereau, then turning north-west across the Île-de-France to Paris and on to the Channel [Britannica Students](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Seine-River/276976) (tier: Established).

**Departments and regions.** The Seine's course is most often said to cross **4 regions** — Bourgogne-Franche-Comté, Grand Est, Île-de-France, Normandie — and **13 departments**: Côte-d'Or, Aube, Marne, Seine-et-Marne, Essonne, Val-de-Marne, Paris, Hauts-de-Seine, Seine-Saint-Denis, Val-d'Oise, Yvelines, Eure, Seine-Maritime [Initiatives Rivers synopsis, PDF](https://www.initiativesrivers.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Synopsis-sheet-SEINE_EN_31012021.pdf) (tier: Established; "400 municipalities in 13 departments") and [Seine en Partage](https://www.seineenpartage.fr/de-villes-en-villes/) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: Medium, tier: Established — advocacy coalition, flagged) give the same 13-department list. A few tallies differ: a 2026-updated Ville de Paris page says the Seine "traverse 12 départements" [Ville de Paris](https://www.paris.fr/pages/la-seine-aurait-plus-de-14-000-ans-et-autres-anecdotes-surprenantes-sur-le-fleuve-parisien-19981) (tier: Primary), presumably omitting the brief Marne touch; and counts reach 14 if the north bank of the estuary mouth at Honfleur (Calvados department) is included, as Sandre-derived commune lists do. Likely difference: whether single touches and estuary-bank departments are counted as "crossed." The **river basin**, distinct from the river's course, is larger: AESN states the Seine-Normandie water agency's basin covers 94,500 km² (18 % of metropolitan France), 28 departments, 6 regions and 8,016 communes, with 18.9 million inhabitants in 2022 (28 % of the French population), Île-de-France concentrating 65 % of basin residents [AESN](https://www.eau-seine-normandie.fr/agence-de-leau/le-bassin-de-la-seine) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary).

### Mouth and estuary

The Seine empties into the **English Channel (la Manche) at the Bay of the Seine (Baie de la Seine)**, between **Le Havre** (Seine-Maritime) on the right bank and **Honfleur** (Calvados) on the left bank — a mouth given identically by AESN, Ville de Paris, Initiatives Rivers and the commune of Rives-en-Seine (all citations above; confidence: High). Le Havre, founded in 1517 to create its port, sits at the mouth [Initiatives Rivers synopsis, PDF](https://www.initiativesrivers.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Synopsis-sheet-SEINE_EN_31012021.pdf) (tier: Established). Where the "river" ends and the "estuary" begins is debated: hydrologists place the estuarine limit at **Poses**, where tidal fluctuation is still felt (a macrotidal estuary, tidal range >2 m, with the salt wedge far downstream), the old administrative maritime limit has varied between Rouen and Berville, while common usage treats the river as running to the estuary mouth at Tancarville near Le Havre [Lestel et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366871446_The_Seine_the_River_dedicated_to_Paris) (tier: Primary). The lower Seine is a major maritime access: some 3,500 seagoing vessels transit the 120 km of channel between Rouen and the sea each year, 22–26 Mt of maritime traffic annually, under compulsory pilotage that sets maximum permissible drafts [Station de Pilotage de la Seine (HAROPA pilots)](https://www.pilote-seine.fr/station/) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary). VNF's official figures classify the Le Havre–Rouen reach at a maximum vessel size of 27,000 t, versus 1,000–5,000 t barges on the "grand gabarit" river upstream [VNF, Les chiffres du transport fluvial 2019, PDF](https://www.vnf.fr/vnf/app/uploads/2020/06/LesChiffresDuTransportFluvial_2019.pdf) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary). Rouen is thus the last port reachable by ocean-going ships and Europe's leading grain-export port complex, while Le Havre handles deep-sea and container traffic (Rouen 22.5 Mt and Le Havre 68.9 Mt in 2015, 91 Mt combined) [ISEMAR Note de synthèse n°185, PDF](https://www.isemar.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/note-de-synth%C3%A8se-isemar-185.pdf) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Established).

### Hydrography: basin, tributaries, discharge, regulation

The Seine's own **catchment** (as distinct from the wider Seine-Normandie agency basin) is given as 75,000 km² by AESN [AESN](https://www.eau-seine-normandie.fr/agence-de-leau/le-bassin-de-la-seine) (tier: Primary), 76,238 km² by Initiatives Rivers (tier: Established, above) and by Lestel et al. at the mouth (65,000 km² at Poses, upstream of the estuary) [Lestel et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366871446_The_Seine_the_River_dedicated_to_Paris) (tier: Primary), ~75,976 km² by the JRC (tier: Primary, above), and 78,000 km² at the mouth (73,700 km² at Tancarville) by Universalis [Universalis](https://www.universalis.fr/encyclopedie/seine/) (tier: Established). Range: **≈75,000–79,000 km²**; the spread reflects whether estuarine fringe and different source datasets are included (flagged below). The JRC sheet also gives Strahler order 7 and mean basin rainfall 666 mm/yr against ~800 mm/yr in Initiatives Rivers' sheet — both are averages over different periods and datasets. The **regime is pluvial-oceanic**: high waters in winter (January–February), low flows in summer (August–September) [Initiatives Rivers](https://www.initiativesrivers.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Synopsis-sheet-SEINE_EN_31012021.pdf) and [Lestel et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366871446_The_Seine_the_River_dedicated_to_Paris) (confidence: High across sources). Groundwater sustains about half of the flow at Poses in the low-flow season [Initiatives Rivers](https://www.initiativesrivers.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Synopsis-sheet-SEINE_EN_31012021.pdf) (tier: Established).

The main **tributaries** are, on the right bank, the Aube, the Marne and the Oise, and on the left bank, the Yonne, the Loing, the Eure and the Risle [Initiatives Rivers](https://www.initiativesrivers.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Synopsis-sheet-SEINE_EN_31012021.pdf) (confidence: High, corroborated by AESN, tier: Primary: Yonne, 293 km, joining at Montereau-Fault-Yonne; Marne, 525 km, joining at Charenton/Alfortville/Ivry just south-east of Paris; Oise, 330 km, rising in Belgium, joining the right bank at Conflans-Sainte-Honorine [AESN](https://www.eau-seine-normandie.fr/agence-de-leau/le-bassin-de-la-seine), accessed 2026-11-27). In all, 47 tributaries feed the Seine [Initiatives Rivers](https://www.initiativesrivers.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Synopsis-sheet-SEINE_EN_31012021.pdf) (tier: Established).

**Discharge** figures vary by gauging station and reference period. In Paris, the City states a normal flow of ~330 m³/s [Ville de Paris](https://www.paris.fr/dossiers/la-seine-coeur-battant-de-paris-216) (tier: Primary); Initiatives Rivers' synopsis gives 310 m³/s in Paris and 563 m³/s at Le Havre (tier: Established); the peer-reviewed Lestel et al. chapter gives a long-term mean of 280 m³/s at Paris-Austerlitz and 485 m³/s at Poses (tier: Primary); Universalis gives 450 m³/s at the mouth on a long-term natural basis, with extremes from a natural minimum of 30 m³/s to a Paris maximum of 2,280 m³/s [Universalis](https://www.universalis.fr/encyclopedie/seine/) (tier: Established); the JRC fact sheet reports ~600 m³/s at Poses and 611 m³/s at the outlet (tier: Primary). This suggests the honest statement is: **on the order of 300 m³/s at Paris and 450–610 m³/s near the mouth**, with the spread explained by averaging periods, the 2001–2010s hydrological series, and the flow-sustaining effect of upstream reservoirs. Summer low flows at Paris can fall to ~150 m³/s despite regulation [Lestel et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366871446_The_Seine_the_River_dedicated_to_Paris) (tier: Primary). Flood control relies on **four reservoir-dams built upstream of Paris from 1949** — Pannecière-Chaumard (Yonne, 80 Mm³), Lac de la Seine (205 Mm³), Lac du Der-Marnant (Marne, 350 Mm³) and Lac d'Aube (170 Mm³), together storing some 805 Mm³ over an area roughly equal to Paris — managed by the inter-departmental agency IIBRBS [Universalis](https://www.universalis.fr/encyclopedie/seine/) (tier: Established) and corroborated by Lestel et al. ("3 main lateral reservoirs," adding 50 m³/s+ of summer baseflow and lowering peak levels by tens of centimetres; tier: Primary). Navigation engineering is equally heavy: 400 km of channelised navigable reach with 29 major locks on the main stem [Lestel et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366871446_The_Seine_the_River_dedicated_to_Paris) (tier: Primary); the Seine basin carried 23.7 Mt of river freight in 2019, the largest of France's inland basins (of 56.3 Mt national total — my computation from [VNF 2019 figures, PDF](https://www.vnf.fr/vnf/app/uploads/2020/06/LesChiffresDuTransportFluvial_2019.pdf), tier: Primary).

### The Seine in Paris: dimensions, banks, islands

Within the commune of Paris the Seine runs **13 km**, is **30 m wide at quai Montebello (5e) to 200 m at the pont de Grenelle (15e)**, has a **depth of 3.40–5.70 m** (deepest at pont Mirabeau, per the City), a normal water height of 1–2 m, and is crossed by **37 bridges, including 4 pedestrian-only footbridges** (the passerelle Simone-de-Beauvoir, opened 2006, is the 37th) — figures repeated consistently by Ville de Paris [La Seine, cœur battant de Paris](https://www.paris.fr/dossiers/la-seine-coeur-battant-de-paris-216), [Paris sous toutes ses longueurs](https://www.paris.fr/pages/paris-sous-toutes-ses-longueurs-7504) and [Paris et ses ponts](https://www.paris.fr/pages/paris-et-ses-ponts-toute-une-histoire-7466) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary) and by the Centre des monuments nationaux's official Sainte-Chapelle/Notre-Dame teaching dossier, which repeats "près de 13 km… trente-sept ponts dont quatre passerelles" [Tours Notre-Dame / Sainte-Chapelle, "La Seine et l'île de la Cité", PDF](https://www.sainte-chapelle.fr/content/download/9873210/file/Dossier%20th%C3%A9matique%20%20La%20Seine%20et%20l'%C3%AEle%20de%20la%20Cit%C3%A9.pdf) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary). (The RATP's consumer site says "38 ponts et 3 passerelles" [Bonjour RATP](https://www.bonjour-ratp.fr/lieux/seine/) — flagged as a minor conflict, tier: Low.)

The river divides the city into the **Rive Droite (Right Bank), north of the Seine, and the Rive Gauche (Left Bank), south of it**. The naming follows the direction of flow, not the compass: bank sides are defined facing downstream, and because the Seine flows east→west through Paris, the north bank is the right bank and the south bank the left [Guichet du Savoir, Bibliothèque municipale de Lyon](https://www.guichetdusavoir.org/question/voir/131235) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Established), confirmed by the official tourism board ("La rive droite rassemble les arrondissements placés au nord de la Seine, la rive gauche comprend ceux du sud" [Paris je t'aime](https://parisjetaime.com/article/se-reperer-a-paris-plan-de-paris-a948), tier: Primary) and by Lonely Planet ("Left Bank and Right Bank refer to the direction of the river's current, which flows westward" [Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/articles/paris-left-bank-versus-right-bank) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Established)). This division is ancient: the BnF (France's national library) describes medieval Paris as already tripartite — the "Cité" (the island, religious and judicial heart), the "Ville" (Right Bank, ports and markets) and the "Université" (Left Bank, student quarter from the Latin Quarter founded around the 12th–13th century) [BnF Essentiels](https://essentiels.bnf.fr/fr/histoire/moyen-age/99f01b1e-76a4-42f6-bfd0-1700e9ebbf99-ville-moyen-age/article/3835a75d-9077-4cd1-95a1-11d9446dcd33-lutece-la-capitale) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary). Paris je t'aime says the Seine borders 9 arrondissements [Paris je t'aime](https://parisjetaime.com/article/se-reperer-a-paris-plan-de-paris-a948) (tier: Primary); the standard list of riverside arrondissements (1er, 4e, 5e, 6e, 7e, 8e, 12e, 13e, 15e, 16e) yields 10, so the count is flagged below.

**The islands.** Two natural islands sit in the heart of the city, both inside the UNESCO property: the **Île de la Cité** and the **Île Saint-Louis** [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/600/) (tier: Primary). The Île de la Cité — today about 22.5 ha, e.g. [RFI Le français facillime teaching dossier, PDF](https://s.francaisfacile.rfi.fr/media/display/0363e8be-a56b-11ee-a500-005056a90284/pvp_b1_seine_corriges.pdf) and [Les Echos](https://www.lesechos.fr/pme-regions/ile-de-france/a-cote-de-notre-dame-de-paris-une-mue-xxl-pour-lile-de-la-cite-2134706) ("un véritable musée vivant sur 22 hectares", fewer than 900 residents, ~30 listed-monument sites) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Established) — is conventionally presented as the birthplace of Paris. Its history is one of human-made consolidation: the City of Paris states that "au début du Ier siècle après J.-C., des îlots de la Seine sont réunis pour former l'actuelle île de la Cité," with Roman quay remains preserved under the parvis at the Crypte archéologique, and that the island was fortified in 308 as Lutèce contracted [Ville de Paris](https://www.paris.fr/pages/l-ile-de-la-cite-deux-mille-ans-de-petites-et-grandes-histoires-8216) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary). The Culture Ministry's archaeology portal cautions that the Gallic oppidum of the Parisii — described by Caesar as "in insula Sequanae" and traditionally identified with the Île de la Cité — has never yielded proven pre-Roman Gallic levels, and that other sites (e.g. Nanterre) are now considered candidates; the Roman-era island was no larger than ~9 ha, about 40 % of today's area [Ministère de la Culture, Paris antique](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/paris/fr/la-lutece-gauloise) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary). The Sainte-Chapelle dossier repeats the 9-hectare ancient extent and notes the first Roman quay was built on infill south of the island in the 1st century [Tours Notre-Dame dossier, PDF](https://www.sainte-chapelle.fr/content/download/9873210/file/Dossier%20th%C3%A9matique%20%20La%20Seine%20et%20l'%C3%AEle%20de%20la%20Cit%C3%A9.pdf) (tier: Primary). Downstream of the Cité lies the **Île Saint-Louis** (~11 ha [RFI teaching dossier, PDF](https://s.francaisfacile.rfi.fr/media/display/0363e8be-a56b-11ee-a500-005056a90284/pvp_b1_seine_corriges.pdf), tier: Established; ~520 m long [Noizet & Gautier, "Paris / La Seine", in *Villes et rivières de France*, CNRS Éditions 2019, PDF](https://hal.science/hal-04014374v1/file/PDF%20Auteurs_Paris.pdf) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary)), which was not naturally one island: it was formed in the 17th century by joining the unoccupied Île Notre-Dame and Île aux Vaches — pastures of the Notre-Dame chapter split by a channel since ~1356 — under a royal contract of 1614 with the entrepreneur Christophe Marie and financiers Poulletier and Le Regrattier, who drained the canal, built the quays and the bridges (the Pont Marie, Pont de la Tournelle) in exchange for lotissement rights; the operation, one of Paris's first large speculative urban projects, was essentially complete by the 1650s [Universalis](https://www.universalis.fr/encyclopedie/ile-saint-louis-paris/) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Established; corroborated by [Paris Musées – Musée Carnavalet](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/musee-carnavalet/oeuvres/vue-de-l-ile-notre-dame), tier: Primary: royal accord 1614, works finished c. 1655, and by the CNRS chapter: "artificiellement formée par la réunion de deux îlots"). A third, narrow artificial island, the **Île aux Cygnes** (15e/16e), carries the Pont de Bir-Hakeim; the CNRS chapter notes all three of today's Paris islands were modified by humans [Noizet & Gautier](https://hal.science/hal-04014374v1/file/PDF%20Auteurs_Paris.pdf) (tier: Primary); the usual 1831 construction date for the Île aux Cygnes was not confirmed in official sources retrieved (see Gaps). The island of L'Île-Saint-Denis, immediately upstream in the suburban loop, is likewise bounded by the river's main channel and its cutting (VNF/IGN mapping would confirm; not independently sourced here).

### Floods, gauging and risk governance in Paris

The Seine's level at Paris is measured permanently at the **Austerlitz gauge (échelle d'Austerlitz)**, whose zero is fixed at 25.92 m NGF-IGN69 (the IGN-referenced national leveling datum tied to the Marseille tide gauge) — a detail showing IGN's datum underpins Paris flood measurement [Ville de Paris, Crues page](https://www.paris.fr/pages/crues-5472) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary; gauge identity confirmed by the national Vigicrues/HydroPortail portal, station F700000103 "La Seine à Paris – Austerlitz" [Vigicrues – HydroPortail/EauFrance](https://www.hydro.eaufrance.fr/stationhydro/F700000103/fiche), accessed 2026-11-27, tier: Primary). Forecasting is the job of the State's **SPC "Seine Moyenne Yonne Loing"** within the Île-de-France environment directorate (DRIEET), with daily levels transmitted to the **Préfecture de Police de Paris**, which runs the city's flood vigilance; the Seine is monitored 365 days a year with four vigilance colours (green/yellow/orange/red) displayed on vigicrues.gouv.fr [Ville de Paris](https://www.paris.fr/pages/crues-5472) (tier: Primary) and [Ville de Paris / EPT Paris Seine Estives flood-management document, PDF](https://episeine.fr/sites/default/files/atoms/files/document_complementaire.pdf) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary). The Préfecture de Police's river brigade (80 agents) patrols the Seine year-round [Ville de Paris](https://www.paris.fr/pages/comment-se-preparer-aux-risques-d-inondations-7276) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary).

The reference event is the **Great January 1910 flood**: peak 8.62 m at Austerlitz on 28 January 1910, a ~7.9 m rise above normal, flooding ~720 ha of Paris over a rise of ~10–15 days and a 35-day recession — the largest overflow since 1658 (whose level, converted, was higher: 8.96 m equivalent) [Episeine — public-interest group for the Seine's culture and development](https://episeine.fr/crues/janvier-1910) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary; corroborated by Ville de Paris, tier: Primary, and VNF's official flood guide, which explains why 1910 — cote 8.62 m, +7.90 m — rather than 1658 (Tournelle gauge 8.81 m) was adopted as the "Plus Hautes Eaux Connues" reference [VNF, Guide crues, PDF](https://www.vnf.fr/vnf/app/uploads/2020/03/Guide-information-recommandations-en-cas-de-crue.pdf), tier: Primary). Reported 1910 peak discharge differs: 2,400 m³/s (Ville de Paris, tier: Primary) versus 2,600 m³/s in Vigicrues' updated historical threshold (set 2017, ±200 m³/s tolerance) [Vigicrues/HydroPortail station thresholds](https://hydro.eaufrance.fr/stationhydro/F700000109/seuils) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary) and in Lestel et al. ("maximum recorded discharge around 2,600," tier: Primary). Recent events for scale: 6.10 m in June 2016, 5.89 m in January 2018, 5.21 m in 2001; navigation is halted from 4.30 m and the low-level quays close from ~3.0–3.2 m [Ville de Paris](https://www.paris.fr/pages/crues-5472) and [Vigicrues bulletin cited in a Ville de Paris crisis-document, PDF](https://eudonetx-scop.apps.paris.fr/XRM/at?cs=eQjJwEV4B_8ZPB0t5rrA5RlJRE8fFoHYb0Lvf9JWc9TpOk7SWUVJeYJsNAmmbUK-&p=MMKsLZHfzqiyhURnyZTBWZtOqDkN-UGgWmFBOYz-Z2B4RVDQFtUqVNhsAG0uaXUC2E83i__MeDs%3D&tok=CVO) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary). The city protects itself with removable flood barriers designed to hold up to the 1910 level, and drills the scenario with the Préfecture de Police (EU SEQUANA 2016 exercise); 182 historical flood-markers (repères de crue) are embedded in buildings across Paris [Ville de Paris flood document, PDF](https://episeine.fr/sites/default/files/atoms/files/document_complementaire.pdf) (tier: Primary).

### World Heritage and present-day status

The Paris banks of the Seine are inscribed on the **UNESCO World Heritage List as "Paris, Banks of the Seine" (ref. 600, inscribed 1991, criteria (i)(ii)(iv); minor boundary modification 2024)**, covering, as the listing currently displays, a 538-ha property with a 3,194-ha buffer zone (figures reflecting the 2024 boundary modification) between the **Pont de Sully and the Pont d'Iéna**, explicitly including the Île de la Cité and Île Saint-Louis. UNESCO's statement of Outstanding Universal Value stresses the river's dual defensive and economic role "from prehistoric times to the Parisii tribes," the upstream/downstream functional split ("upstream, the port and river transport; downstream, royal and aristocratic Paris"), and State ownership of the quays as fluvial public domain [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/600/) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary). Present-day relevance: after a large clean-up programme, the Seine hosted the Paris 2024 Olympics triathlon and open-water swimming events and opened to public swimming from summer 2025 at three sites in Paris, with a political debate under way on granting the river legal rights [Ville de Paris](https://www.paris.fr/dossiers/la-seine-coeur-battant-de-paris-216) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: High, tier: Primary). The City also popularises research suggesting the Seine's present course in the Paris area is roughly 14,000 years old [Ville de Paris](https://www.paris.fr/pages/la-seine-aurait-plus-de-14-000-ans-et-autres-anecdotes-surprenantes-sur-le-fleuve-parisien-19981) (accessed 2026-11-27, confidence: Medium, tier: Primary — the underlying peer-reviewed study was not retrieved; a CNRS chapter documents Pleistocene palaeo-meanders north of the present course, one 8–8.5 km long from l'Arsenal to Chaillot, whose drained bed shaped the Right Bank's street pattern [Noizet & Gautier](https://hal.science/hal-04014374v1/file/PDF%20Auteurs_Paris.pdf), tier: Primary — which supports the broader point).
